WHY THE 794 nm CLAIM DOES NOT SURVIVE
--------------------------------------
Full min/max inspection shows nearly every filter has a large NEGATIVE
excursion in the 606-623 nm band that is LARGER IN MAGNITUDE than its
positive bump near 794 nm. The original text's "~794 nm" cluster comes
from taking only the signed (positive) maximum, which ignores these
larger negative troughs. Example (pairwise Filter 1): positive max
147.27 at 794.2 nm, but negative min -266.67 at 616.0 nm -- the true
|activation| peak is at 616.0 nm, not 794.2 nm.

Recomputing with argmax(|activation|) for all 32 filters (16 pairwise +
16 triplet):
  Filters with true peak in the red-edge region (>=780 nm): 0 of 16 (pairwise), 0 of 16 (triplet)
  Filters with true peak in the 599.7-623.5 nm band:        16 of 16 (pairwise), 15 of 16 (triplet)
  Remaining triplet filter (Filter 9): true peak at the boundary row (800.7 nm, see above).

VERDICT: The ~794 nm / red-edge engagement claim does NOT survive
criterion-correct re-analysis. Zero filters in either network have their
true largest-magnitude activation there. It is a sign-selection artifact
of the original signed-max method, not a real second engagement region
(and not, strictly, a padding artifact either, since row 54 itself is a
valid interior row) -- do not use it in the reviewer response. Rest the
argument on the 606-623 nm onset region alone, which is supported by
31 of 32 filters across both networks.
